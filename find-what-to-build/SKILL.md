# Find What To Build

The opportunity is never "what's painful" or "what sells" alone. It's the **overlap of
three circles**. Build only in the intersection; one or two circles is a trap.

## The three circles
1. **Pay-willingness** — what people already pay for (freelance marketplaces are a free,
   validated demand signal: thousands of orders for a gig = real pain + real budget).
2. **Real pain** — what genuinely hurts (surveys, communities, your own customers). For
   SMBs in 2026: reaching customers / growing sales (#1), hiring/retention, rising costs,
   owners stuck in the day-to-day, compliance.
3. **Reliably automatable today** — what agents actually do in production: support triage,
   CRM logging/notes, lead outreach + qualification, content generation, doc/invoice
   processing. (Not: filing taxes, moving money, hiring/firing, anything one hallucination
   makes a lawsuit.)

The intersection of all three = a real business. Examples that hit all three: sales
outreach / appointment-setting agents, done-for-you marketing content, tier-1 support.

## Verdict scale for any task
- ✅ agent can own it now · ⚠️ agent drafts, human approves/files · ❌ judgment/liability,
  don't. Anything ❌ becomes ⚠️ only with a human gate — never autonomous.

## The "100 agents" trap
Don't build 100 shallow agents (or a 28-subsystem everything-platform). That's fragile
everywhere and paid-for nowhere. **Build narrow and deep:** one department × one painful,
paid-for, automatable workflow, done well enough that ONE real business pays. Then expand
to the next square. Departments are a *map*, not a build plan.

## How to find real problems (you don't have to guess)
1. **Read freelance job posts** (Upwork/Fiverr buyer requests) in a category — validated
   demand with budget, free to read.
2. **Do the work manually / freelance it first** — fastest way to learn the real workflow;
   then automate the parts that hurt. Instant revenue too.
3. **Go where owners complain** — niche subreddits, Indie Hackers, local business groups.
4. **Mom-test interviews** — ask what wasted their time *last week*; never "would you buy…".
5. **Start with a niche you can reach** — local + specific beats global + generic for a
   first customer.

## The killer move
Don't build then hunt for customers. Find a customer with a painful, recurring,
currently-paid-for task; do it manually (or as a freelancer); then absorb it into an agent.
The freelance platform is your demand-validation layer.

## The one line
Build where pay-willingness, real pain, and reliable automation overlap — narrow and deep,
validated by one paying customer before you scale.
