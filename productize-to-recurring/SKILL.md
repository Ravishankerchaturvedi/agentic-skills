# Productize to Recurring

The most common founder mistake: pricing a **recurring-value** product as a **one-time
project**. An agent that works every month should earn every month.

## The core fix: separate the three money lines
1. **Setup / build fee** (one-time) — your labour to stand it up.
2. **Recurring** (monthly) — the real MRR: hosting + maintenance + improvements + the
   learning loop (the thing that compounds = the thing they pay for monthly).
3. **Running cost** (usage) — telephony/voice/LLM. The client pays this (BYO keys or you
   bill-through with markup). **State it explicitly** or you go underwater when they scale.

A one-time fee + tiny annual AMC = a consulting gig you must keep re-selling. Recurring =
a business that compounds.

## The tiered ladder (good → better → best)
Map tiers to architecture + value, and price the *value*, not the effort:
1. Managed agent — cheap setup, but highest running cost for the client.
2. Custom agent + premium voice — your control; lower running cost.
3. + self-learning loop — **the moat; price it recurring**, it improves every month.
4. + CRM/brain/voice full product — top tier.
Mistake to avoid: pricing the learning loop (your moat) as a one-time add-on. It's the
recurring justification.

## Build once, sell config many times
Build the platform ONCE; each new client is **configuration**, not new dev. So setup fees
reflect config effort; recurring reflects ongoing value + platform upkeep. Margins are fat
only if you don't rebuild per client.

## White-label (the B2B2B scale move)
Three flavors, ascending infra:
1. **Done-for-you under their brand** — you run it, they brand reports. Near-zero new
   infra. **Start here.**
2. **Branded self-serve platform** — rebranded dashboard + sub-accounts (the GoHighLevel
   model). Most infra: multi-tenant isolation, theming, reseller admin, usage billing.
3. **API/embed** — they bake it into their product.

White-label pricing: setup + **monthly platform license** (your MRR) + **wholesale usage**
(you mark up raw cost; they retail to their customers). Keep the **learning loop as your
layer** — it's why they can't easily leave and why their agents improve.

Caution: white-label multiplies your reliability/SLA burden and it's *their* brand that
suffers if it breaks. **Stabilize with direct clients first**; don't white-label an
unstable product, and don't build the full multi-tenant platform before the model is proven.

## The relationship rule (first customers, mentors, friends)
Honor the price you quoted; don't re-trade. Make the founder-rate up on cold customers +
running-cost markup + future deals. And make any pre-sold claim true fast (a real demo) —
sell the vision, then deliver it; don't fabricate a fake case study you must keep covering.

## The one line
Setup + monthly + usage, priced on value; build once and configure; white-label only after
it's stable; keep the learning loop yours.
